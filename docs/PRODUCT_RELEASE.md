# Product scope

MemeHelm combines pons v2 issuance/trading on Robinhood mainnet with creator operations and transparent ETH revenue routing. The product is creator-led: AI supplies drafts and suggestions, while the creator approves public updates and signs transactions.

The launch flow offers a direct EOA fee recipient, a MemeHelm fixed two-wallet split, a holders vault, or a community escrow as the pons fee recipient. Supply, graduation rules and base fees come from enabled pons configurations. Optional creator fees cannot exceed the live cap. The wizard can exempt up to 32 chosen wallets from pons' opening anti-snipe fee: the current snipe window and starting rate are read from the factory, the list is validated in the browser, at preparation and on save, and it is fixed onchain at launch. Launches without exemptions use the factory's standard three-argument launch call unchanged. There is no arbitrary premint, independent factory activation, initial vault deposit disguised as a buy, automated trading or cross-chain flow.

The holders vault shares collected creator fees with token holders. The workspace prepares each payout round from one Blockscout balance snapshot: shares are pro-rata within the returned balances, protocol contracts (bonding curve, pool manager, the vault itself) are excluded, balances that would pay under 0.001 ETH are dropped and the round pays at most 200 wallets. The prepared transaction lists every recipient and amount; the creator wallet signs the single distribute() call and the onchain contract executes exactly that list. Onchain code cannot verify the explorer snapshot, so the UI states this bound explicitly; rounds are all-or-nothing and final once signed, and none of the three vaults has received an independent audit.

The community escrow holds collected fees behind a public review window. Every release starts as an onchain proposal that anyone can read on the explorer, waits a fixed two-day window, and is then released by a second signed transaction; open proposals can be cancelled. Release transactions are prepared only while the project's X connection is active, tying payouts to a social identity the workspace has verified through OAuth while never taking custody of it. Onchain rules cannot read social identities; the UI discloses that the window and the signed proposal are the only onchain guarantees.

Discovery and trade history cover recent 20,000 blocks, up to 30 launch cards and 60 recent trades. Charts use actual execution events or locally observed spot prices, with source/coverage labels. Read-only custom-pair markets retain the actual quote asset and decimals, including USDG; transaction preparation still accepts native ETH pairs only. Core facts load independently of holder indexing and trade history, with bounded deadlines and retry. There is no all-time indexer, fabricated history, USD oracle or investment rating. Holder data depends on Blockscout availability.

Token pages include an X-Ray tab with basic onchain forensics: pons v2 registration and phase, creator fee economics with the current fee recipient and its contract/wallet type, supply concentration from the largest returned Blockscout balances, observed buy/sell counts and values within the covered blocks, and trades sent directly by the launching wallet. Every check states its coverage window and missing data stays unavailable rather than estimated. X-Ray produces no safety score or rating and is labeled as a basic read of public chain data, not an independent audit. The same read surfaces material fact flags beside the token name — the creator fee rate, fees routed to a contract, top-balance concentration, in-window trades by the launching wallet, and non-pons issuance — as plainly labeled facts, never as a warning count or safety score.

A Cmd/Ctrl+K search palette reaches every workspace page, the signed-in user's own projects, and the recent indexed launches, and it accepts a raw 0x address to open a token directly. Search results only cover what the product actually indexes — there is no all-time token directory behind them.

The launch wizard embeds Copilot where decisions are made. From a one-sentence idea it drafts up to three candidate names, symbols and stories for the creator to apply or discard, and on the economics step it suggests an optional creator fee with an honest rationale, bounded by the live protocol cap. Suggestions respect the wallet-level AI request allowance, are validated before display, and are drafts only: the creator edits and approves every value that goes onchain. After a verified launch is saved, Copilot drafts one first operations brief automatically; if the allowance or service is unavailable, the launch flow is unaffected. AI-generated text never becomes public without explicit publication.

The Copilot workspace includes a project chat for the signed-in owner. Each answer is assembled server-side from the same observed inputs as the rest of the product: the chain snapshot, X-Ray forensics, the most recent trades and top returned balances within 20,000 blocks, and the saved project memory with open actions and recorded outcomes. The model is instructed to answer only from those facts, to say when data is missing instead of estimating, and to mention the coverage window when citing observed data; the reply carries the same coverage note back to the UI. Conversations are stored in the wallet's own local workspace — the server replays what it actually recorded, never what a request claims the assistant previously said — bounded to twenty saved chats per project and forty messages per chat, with the last twelve turns used as context. An Open chats panel lists, reopens and deletes them, and a Live observations sidebar shows the project token's observed price, phase and progress, holder count, largest-balance concentration and in-window trade counts, each stamped with its observation time and coverage. Chats never leave the private workspace and consume the wallet-level AI allowance plus the project's daily AI request limit. Chat is read-only by design: it cannot send transactions, publish posts, access wallets or social accounts, and it gives no investment advice, price predictions or trading instructions.

Creator operations now includes a Today overview, contribution goals, a private content calendar and a local Meme Studio. Goals use separately approved public snapshots, wallet-authenticated submissions, creator review, optional public entries and nonfinancial community points. Withdrawals remove public entries and points; completed goals can carry an explicitly shared factual recap. Content plans and creator-recorded results inform later AI reviews. Meme Studio composes the user's local artwork and captions into a downloadable PNG, without AI image generation or media publishing. Completed task results can be shared separately from private instructions. Market evidence retains observation times and explorer-sample limits. See `GROWTH_WORKSPACE.md` for workflow and acceptance boundaries.

EIP-4361 sign-in, chain/wallet-scoped browser drafts, wallet-owned SQLite records and explicit publication protect private work. The server binds to loopback. Hosting, production operations and a published security audit have not been supplied/completed. User-facing pages contain product actions and material transaction facts, not developer progress or setup instructions.
